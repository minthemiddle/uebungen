NOTES

(1) Marco, che è arrivato ieri: Marco, who arrived yesterday; Marco,
che hai conosciuto a Roma: Marco, whom you met in Rome.
Who. whom and which are always che in Italian. However, Chi é
venuto?: Who came? Chi is an interrogative pronoun.

(2) Una settimana fa: a week ago; due anni fa: two years ago. Fa,
essentially ago, always foliows phrases indicating time.

(3) Un uomo, una donna: a man, a woman. But: Ho due gatti, un
maschio e una femmina: | have two kittens, a male and a female.
Also: Chiara ha avuto un figlio. - E' un maschio o una femmina?:
Chiara has given birth to a child. - Is ita boy or a girl? Thus, one
uses maschio and femmina only for animals and babies. Men
and women are never referred to as males and females.

(4) Scrivo loro or gli scrivo: in spoken Italian, there's a tendency to
use g/i for the masculine or feminine plural, the use of /oro being
limited to the written and formal language (aiso notice /oro
always follows the verb).

(5) Auguri! Tanti auguri! (Literally: wishes, lots of wishes) It's the
most common formula Italians use for a birthday, Christmas, a
wedding and other wishes.
